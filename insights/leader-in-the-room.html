Facilitation

The person who runs the meeting cannot also decide it

A leadership offsite fails in a predictable way. The most senior person opens the day, watches the clock, pulls quiet people in, and then is asked for the decision. Those are two jobs. They do not fit in one chair.

When the leader facilitates, the room edits itself. People wait to hear the answer. Disagreement gets polite. The agenda finishes. The choice does not.

An outside facilitator does not own the strategy. They own the queue, the time, and the board. The leader stays in the conversation. That is the entire point of hiring one.

Hire one when the day has to end in a decision: quarterly planning, a strategy reset, a first offsite with a new leadership team, two groups that have not agreed. Do not hire one for a status meeting.
